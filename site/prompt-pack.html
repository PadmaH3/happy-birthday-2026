00 Before the first edit

Upscale the master PNG 2× to about 2804×2244 and derive everything from that one file. Two reasons: 1402px will visibly soften on a laptop screen, and video generators have far more to hold onto at 2.8k. Do it once, up front — every plate must share a single common ancestor or they won't composite cleanly.

All coordinates below are given in the original 1402×1122 space. If you upscale 2×, double them. They're approximate on purpose — pad your selections generously, since the compositing step will crop back to what actually changed.

01 Three edits become five plates

You never generate a whole plate. You generate three isolated regions — left window shut, right window shut, the blush — and paste them onto the untouched master in different combinations. Everything outside those regions stays byte-identical across all five plates, which is the entire ballgame: it's what stops the wall from shimmering when the page cross-fades.

05 Edit C — the blush

This is the payoff of the entire site, and the one edit worth re-rolling until it's right. The failure mode to watch for is character drift: a face that is almost the same person reads as deeply wrong when it cross-fades.

She looks up region ≈ x 975–1150, y 430–640 
Edit only the woman's head, shoulders and hands in the right-hand window.

She looks up from her book and turns her head toward the left of the frame — toward the other window across the wall. Her eyes open behind the round glasses and glance in that direction, slightly upward. Her chin lifts off her supporting hand, and that hand drifts up to rest lightly against her cheek.

Give her a warm rosy flush high on both cheeks and lightly across the bridge of her nose: a soft watercolour bloom that bleeds outward into the skin tone, never a hard-edged circle or a painted-on dot.

Add a small, involuntary, caught-out smile — lips closed or barely parted, corners tucked up. Shy and private, not broad or grinning. She has just been caught looking at someone.

Critical: she must remain unmistakably the same character. Preserve her exact face shape and proportions, the weight and character of the ink lines describing her features, the top-knot bun, the bindi, the round wire glasses and the maroon kurta.

Keep completely unchanged: the open book on the sill, the bird, the windowsill, the shutters, the window frame, the bougainvillea, and the lighting.

06 The four clips

Use a generator that accepts both a first and a last frame — Kling and Luma both do this well, Runway too. Feed it the exact plates. That's what keeps each clip landing precisely on the next state instead of drifting somewhere near it.

The locked camera line is not optional. AI video adds a slow push or drift by default, and any camera movement at all breaks registration with the still plate underneath — the window would float away from its own wall. It's in every prompt below. Leave it in.

For the breeze loop, give it the same frame as first and last, generate about five seconds, and I'll play it ping-pong — forward then reversed. That makes the loop seamless by construction, so you don't have to fight for a perfect cycle.

07 Check before you hand them over

Flip test the plates. Open all five and toggle between them fast. Only the windows and her face should move. If the wall texture crawls or a vine jumps, the compositing slipped — realign rather than regenerate.

Freeze-frame every clip. Compare frame one against its source plate and the final frame against its destination plate. Any zoom or drift and it's a re-roll — this is the single most common failure.

Watch her face in clip 4 at full size. If she becomes a slightly different person partway through, that clip is unusable no matter how pretty the motion is.

Export sanely. Plates as PNG at full size. Clips as MP4 / H.264, no audio track, no wider than 1600px. The finished page has a hard 16MB ceiling, so if the breeze loop lands over about 4MB, trim its length before you trim its quality.

08 Outpaint to a 21:9 panorama

The cinematic desktop crop currently works by cropping the existing 1.24:1 art down to 21:9 — centred on the windows, it loses the roofline and the pots. This is the other direction: extend the canvas so the scene is natively wide enough that nothing has to be cropped away. More wall, more garden, same two windows, now sitting inside a fuller establishing shot.

The math, using your 2304×1856 master: to reach 21:9 at the full 1856px height with nothing cropped, the canvas needs to be about 4331px wide — roughly 1013px of new content on each side. That's a lot to ask in one pass. If your tool struggles, add less (400–600px a side just for breathing room) rather than pushing for the full math in one go.

Extend left and right canvas: 2304×1856 → ≈4330×1856 
This is a canvas extension of an existing illustration, not a re-illustration. Preserve the entire existing image exactly as it is, pixel for pixel: do not redraw, recolour, relight, restyle, or shift anything currently in frame, including both windows, the man, the woman, the bird, the bougainvillea, the palm fronds, the terracotta pots, and the roofline.

Extend the canvas by approximately 1000px on the left and 1000px on the right, so the finished image reads as a wide cinematic panorama with the two windows sitting inside a fuller view of the building's facade and garden, rather than filling the whole frame edge to edge.

New content, left side: continue the same warm yellow-ochre stucco wall with its mottled watercolour washes, the same soft grey dappled shadows, and the same climbing bougainvillea with its pink and coral blossoms trailing further along the wall. Continue the terracotta roofline and its tile pattern across the top of the new area. Near the outer edge, let the wall give way — as it already begins to at the current left edge — to a glimpse of a neighbouring rooftop and a sliver of pale sky, so the eye understands the building ends there. Add one more terracotta pot with a simple potted plant near the base, echoing the pots already in frame.

New content, right side: continue the same stucco wall, roofline, tile pattern, and bougainvillea vine. Continue the palm fronds draping in from the upper right, letting more of the palm tree reveal itself. Continue the stone paving or low steps already hinted at in the bottom right corner, and add one or two more terracotta pots along it.

Throughout the new areas: the same loose pen-and-ink outlines, the same watercolour paper texture, the same warm sunlight falling from the upper left, the same palette. The seam between old and new must be invisible — no hard edge, no shift in colour temperature, no change in line weight.

Do not add any new people, animals, windows, doors, or text.

Which tool, and how to split it

  • Photoshop Generative Expand (recommended): resize the canvas first (Image → Canvas Size, extend both sides by the numbers above), select the new empty areas, and run Generative Fill with this prompt once. It's built for exactly this and keeps the seam cleanest since it can see and match both edges of real pixels at once.
  • ChatGPT / Gemini / other chat-based editors: if the tool only extends one side per pass, split this into two separate jobs — run the preamble paragraph plus only the "left side" paragraph first, save that result, then feed that file back in and run the preamble plus only the "right side" paragraph. Each pass then only ever has to match one clean edge, not two.

This edit is bigger and riskier than the plate edits — you're asking for roughly 44% more canvas per side. Expect to run it more than once, and check the seam closely at full zoom before calling it done; a faint band at the join is the usual failure mode and sometimes needs a small manual levels touch-up to disappear completely.

09 Plates for a Midjourney panorama

If you outpainted in Midjourney instead, you now have a beautiful wide scene — but Midjourney regenerates rather than preserving pixels, so it's a new master, not an extension of the old one. It won't crossfade against the five original plates. It needs its own small set of edits, same idea as section 01–05, just re-pointed at the new image.

The panorama itself is already Wide‑P1, both closed — a single grid of panes fills each arch, with just a faint silhouette behind the glass. That's also the state the page loads on by default, so it needs no edit at all. Only two AI edits are left; the third plate comes free by compositing the first two together.

Recommended split: keep the original five plates for mobile — that tight, intimate framing is arguably better on a phone anyway. Use this new wide master only for the desktop cinematic view. Two asset sets, one for each shape, rather than forcing one image to do both jobs.
